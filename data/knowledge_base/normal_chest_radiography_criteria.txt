CRITERIA FOR NORMAL CHEST RADIOGRAPHY:
A normal adult posteroanterior (PA) chest radiograph exhibits clear pulmonary parenchymal aeration throughout both lungs, without evidence of focal consolidations, interstitial reticular opacities, ground-glass opacification, or parenchymal masses. The bilateral pulmonary vascular markings should taper smoothly from the central hilar regions toward the peripheral lung margins, without vascular congestion or cephalization.

Both costophrenic and cardiophrenic angles must be sharply delineated and acute, indicating absence of significant pleural fluid accumulation or fibrothorax. The diaphragmatic domes should be smoothly convex with the right hemidiaphragm typically positioned 1 to 2 cm higher than the left due to the underlying liver. Clear subdiaphragmatic structures include the gastric bubble on the left and absence of free subdiaphragmatic pneumoperitoneum.

The cardiac silhouette should demonstrate a normal cardiothoracic ratio (CTR) under 0.50 on full inspiratory PA projections. The aortic arch, aortopulmonary window, and superior mediastinal contours should be sharp and non-widened. The hilar structures should demonstrate normal symmetrical density and branching morphology without hilar enlargement or lymphadenopathy.

The thoracic bony cage, including bilateral clavicles, ribs, sternum, and visible thoracic vertebral bodies, should display intact cortical outlines without acute fractures, lytic lesions, or blastic bony metastases. Soft tissues of the chest wall should be symmetrical without subcutaneous emphysema.

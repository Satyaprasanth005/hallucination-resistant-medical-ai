ALVEOLAR CONSOLIDATION AND PNEUMONIA:
Consolidation represents the replacement of alveolar air by exudate, pus, blood, or cells. Key radiographic features include confluent, homogeneous or patchy acinar opacification that obscures underlying pulmonary vascular margins. The presence of 'air bronchograms' (radiolucent branching bronchi projecting through opacified parenchyma) is definitive evidence of alveolar space filling. The 'silhouette sign' occurs when an intrathoracic opacity abuts a cardiovascular border of similar density, causing loss of the normal radiographic interface (e.g. right middle lobe consolidation silhouetting the right heart border).

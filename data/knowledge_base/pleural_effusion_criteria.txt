RADIOLOGICAL CRITERIA FOR PLEURAL EFFUSION:
Pleural effusion manifests on upright frontal chest radiographs as blunting of the lateral and posterior costophrenic angles. An upright PA view typically requires approximately 175-200 mL of pleural fluid to cause blunted lateral costophrenic sulci. Characteristic radiographic features include a homogeneous lower thoracic opacity with a concave superior border termed the 'meniscus sign'. In supine radiographs, fluid layers posteriorly, producing a diffuse ground-glass haziness across the hemithorax without obscuring vascular markings. Massive effusions produce contralateral mediastinal shift.

RADIOLOGICAL MANIFESTATIONS OF PNEUMONIA AND INFECTIOUS PROCESSES:
Pneumonia manifests on chest radiographs primarily through alveolar airspace filling, visible as homogeneous or patchy pulmonary opacifications and consolidations. Pathognomonic signs include air bronchograms—tubular lucencies representing patent air-filled bronchial trees surrounded by fluid-filled, opacified alveoli—which definitively localize the disease process to the pulmonary parenchyma rather than the pleural space.

The anatomic distribution aids in identifying the infectious etiology:
1. Lobar pneumonia typically produces dense, contiguous opacification sharply bounded by visceral interlobar fissures (classic Streptococcus pneumoniae).
2. Bronchopneumonia manifests as multifocal, patchy, non-segmental opacities centered around terminal bronchioles, often seen with Staphylococcus aureus or Gram-negative bacilli.
3. Interstitial / atypical pneumonia (Mycoplasma, viral infections) presents with bilateral reticular or reticulonodular interstitial markings and peribronchial cuffing, typically disproportionately prominent relative to physical auscultation findings.

The Silhouette Sign provides precise lobar localization:
- Loss of the right heart border indicates right middle lobe (medial segment) consolidation.
- Loss of the left heart border indicates lingular segment (left upper lobe) involvement.
- Loss of the right hemidiaphragmatic contour indicates right lower lobe consolidation.
- Loss of the descending thoracic aortic stripe indicates left lower lobe consolidation.
- Loss of the superior mediastinal or aortic knob contour indicates upper lobe involvement.

Complications of pneumonia to inspect vigilantly include parapneumonic effusion, empyema, lung abscess formation (characterized by an air-fluid level within a thick cavitary wall), and necrotizing pneumonia with pneumatocele development.

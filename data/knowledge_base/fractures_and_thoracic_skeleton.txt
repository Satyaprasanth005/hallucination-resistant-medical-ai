EVALUATION OF THORACIC SKELETAL INJURIES AND RIB FRACTURES:
Systematic interrogation of the thoracic skeleton on chest radiography requires careful tracking of the cortical margins of all visualized clavicles, anterior and posterior rib arcs, scapulae, sternum, and thoracic vertebral bodies. Cortical disruption, step-off deformity, offset alignment, or overlapping bony fragments indicate acute structural fracture.

Rib fractures represent the most prevalent thoracic injury following blunt trauma. Posterior rib fractures carry heightened biomechanical significance and may be complicated by underlying pulmonary contusion, pneumothorax, or hemothorax. First and second rib fractures require high-energy trauma and suggest potential injury to the underlying subclavian vessels, aortic arch, or brachial plexus. Fractures of the lower ribs (ribs 9 through 12) mandate vigilant evaluation for occult intra-abdominal visceral injury, particularly splenic laceration on the left or hepatic trauma on the right.

Flail chest occurs when three or more consecutive ribs are fractured in two or more separate anatomical locations, creating an unstable, paradoxical chest wall segment during respiration. This results in severe respiratory compromise and underlying parenchymal pulmonary contusion.

Subcutaneous emphysema (dissecting air lucencies within chest wall soft tissues) often accompanies rib fractures with underlying pleural laceration. Sternal fractures are best evaluated on lateral projections, where sternal cortical step-offs and anterior retrosternal hematomas are readily depicted.
